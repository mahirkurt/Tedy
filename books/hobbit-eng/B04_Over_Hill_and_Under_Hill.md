## Chapter IV
## Over Hill and Under Hill

There were many paths that led up into the Misty Mountains. There were also many passes over them. But most of the paths were false. They led nowhere or to bad places. Most of the passes were full of evil things and great dangers.

The dwarves and the hobbit had the wise advice of Elrond and the knowledge of Gandalf to help them. Even so, the mountains were hard and high. The path they followed went up and up. It became steeper and more difficult. The air grew colder. The trees became smaller and fewer.

At last they came to the high passes. The wind blew strongly. Clouds covered the tops of the mountains. One day a great storm came. Thunder crashed. Lightning flashed. Rain and hail fell hard. The ponies were frightened. The company could not go on.

They looked for shelter. At last they found a dry cave in the side of the mountain. It seemed large enough and safe. They led the ponies inside. They made a small fire near the entrance. They were tired and wet and glad to rest.

They posted a watch. Then most of them fell asleep. Bilbo tried to sleep too, but he felt uneasy. The cave seemed too comfortable. Something about it troubled him.

In the middle of the night Bilbo woke suddenly. He heard a noise. It was a crack. Then another. The back wall of the cave was opening! A great crack appeared in the rock. Through the crack poured a host of goblins.

The goblins were many and quick. They rushed into the cave with a noise of shouting and clattering. Before the dwarves could fully wake and fight, the goblins had seized them. The ponies screamed and fled out into the storm. The goblins grabbed the luggage and the travellers. They dragged everyone down into the dark tunnels under the mountain.

Bilbo was carried along with the others. The tunnels were black, wet and full of bad smells. The goblins laughed and sang ugly songs as they marched. They went deeper and deeper under the mountain.

At last they came to a great open space. It was a big cave hall. In the middle sat the Great Goblin. He was larger than the others. He had a huge head and cruel eyes. He sat on a flat stone and looked at the prisoners with hate.

“Who are these miserable persons?” he roared.

The goblins pushed Thorin forward. Thorin stood as straight as he could and answered. He said they were simply travellers who had taken shelter from the storm. He did not tell the Great Goblin their real names or their true purpose.

The Great Goblin was not satisfied. He ordered a search of their bags. The goblins found the swords from the troll cave. When the Great Goblin saw Orcrist and Glamdring he became furious. He knew those swords. They were famous goblin-killers from the old wars.

“Kill them all!” he shouted. “Kill them now!”

At that moment Gandalf, who had been missing, suddenly appeared. He had a bright blue light on the tip of his staff. He called out in a loud voice and the light flashed. In the confusion he struck the Great Goblin with his sword. The Great Goblin fell down dead.

The other goblins howled with rage and fear. Chaos broke out. Gandalf called to the dwarves to follow him. He led them in a wild race through the dark tunnels. The goblins ran after them, shouting and waving their weapons.

The company ran as hard as they could. The tunnels twisted and turned. Sometimes they went up, sometimes down. The goblins were close behind. Bilbo was the smallest and the slowest. Dori often carried him on his back.

They ran for a long time. At last they seemed to be leaving the main goblin halls behind. The noise of the pursuers grew a little fainter. But the danger was not over.

Quite suddenly Dori, who was at the back again carrying Bilbo, was grabbed from behind in the dark. He shouted and fell. The hobbit rolled off his shoulders into the blackness. Bilbo bumped his head on hard rock and remembered nothing more.
