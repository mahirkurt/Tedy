## Chapter V
## Riddles in the Dark

When Bilbo opened his eyes, he wondered if he really had opened them. It was just as dark as when they were shut. No one was near him. He felt only the cold stone of the floor.

Very slowly he got up. He felt about on his hands and knees until he touched the wall of the tunnel. He could not tell which way was up or down the tunnel. He was lost.

He felt in his pockets. Most of them were empty. His little knife was still there. Then his hand touched something hard and cold and round. It was a ring. He put it in his pocket without thinking much about it. He did not know it was a magic ring.

Bilbo was very frightened. He was alone under the mountain, far from the dwarves and Gandalf. He wanted to cry, but he told himself he must be brave. He was a hobbit, and hobbits are stronger than they look.

He began to walk carefully along the tunnel, feeling the wall with one hand. The tunnel went down and down. The air grew colder and damper. After a long time he heard a sound. It was a soft, slimy sound, like something wet moving in the dark.

“Bless us and splash us, my precious!” said a thin, unpleasant voice. “What is it?”

Bilbo stopped. He could see two big round pale eyes in the dark. They were looking at him.

The creature that belonged to the eyes was Gollum. Gollum lived alone deep under the mountain. He was small and thin and dark. He had large pale eyes that could see in the dark. He lived on fish and the small creatures he could catch. Sometimes he caught goblins too, when he was lucky. He talked to himself, calling himself “my precious”.

Gollum was very hungry. He saw Bilbo and thought of food.

“Who are you?” hissed Gollum.

“I am Mr. Bilbo Baggins,” said Bilbo, trying to sound brave. “I have lost the dwarves and I want to get out.”

Gollum did not care about the dwarves. He cared about eating. But Bilbo had a sword (his little knife seemed larger and brighter in the dark). Gollum was not sure he could attack safely. So he suggested a game.

“We will play riddles,” said Gollum. “If you ask me a riddle and I cannot guess it, I will show you the way out. If I ask you a riddle and you cannot guess it, then I will eat you.”

Bilbo did not like the idea, but he had no choice. He agreed.

They asked each other many riddles. Some were old and well-known. Some were harder. Bilbo answered Gollum’s riddles. Gollum answered Bilbo’s riddles. They were both clever at this game.

At last Gollum asked a very hard riddle. Bilbo could not think of the answer. He sat in the dark and grew more and more afraid. Time was running out. Then, without really meaning to, he put his hand in his pocket and felt the ring. He said the first words that came into his head:

“What have I got in my pocket?”

Gollum thought this was not a fair riddle. But Bilbo insisted. Gollum had three guesses. He guessed wrong three times. According to the rules, Bilbo had won.

Gollum was furious. He pretended he would still show Bilbo the way out. But first he went back to his little island on the dark lake to get something. That something was his magic ring. Gollum used the ring to become invisible when he hunted.

When Gollum reached the island he found the ring was gone. He became wild with rage and fear. He guessed that Bilbo had it. He came back in a terrible state, meaning to kill the hobbit and take the ring back.

Bilbo put the ring on, not knowing what it would do. At once he became invisible. Gollum could not see him. Gollum ran past him, sniffing and cursing, and went up the tunnel that led to the goblin gate.

Bilbo followed quietly. He did not dare take the ring off. He followed Gollum all the way to the gate. There were many goblins there, guarding the door that led out of the mountain. Gollum stopped and hid. He was too afraid of the goblins to go near the gate.

Bilbo waited. When the chance came, he slipped past the goblins. They could not see him because of the ring. They felt something move, but they could not catch him. Bilbo ran out into the open air at last.

He had escaped from the mountain. He still had the ring on his finger. He did not yet understand how important it was. But he was free, and the sun was shining.
