## Chapter VI
## Out of the Frying-Pan into the Fire

Bilbo had escaped the goblins, but he did not know where he was. He had lost his hood, his cloak, his food, his pony, his buttons and his friends. He walked on and on. The sun began to go down behind the mountains. Shadows fell across his path.

He looked back and then forward. He saw that he was on a path that led down the mountain. Suddenly he heard voices. He hid behind a rock and listened.

The voices belonged to the dwarves and Gandalf. They were arguing. Some of them thought Bilbo was dead. Others thought he had run away. Gandalf was angry with them for leaving the hobbit behind.

Bilbo put on the ring and became invisible. He crept close. Then he took the ring off and said in a loud voice:

“And here is Mr. Bilbo Baggins himself, at your service!”

The dwarves jumped in surprise. They were very glad to see him. They asked how he had escaped. Bilbo told them some of the story, but he did not mention the ring. He felt it was better to keep that secret for now.

They had little time to talk. The goblins had discovered that the prisoners had escaped. Soon the mountains were full of searching goblins. Even worse, packs of wolves (wargs) began to gather in the woods below. The wargs and the goblins often worked together.

Gandalf told the company to run. They ran down the slopes as fast as they could. But the wargs were faster. Soon the wolves were all around them. The company climbed up into the trees to escape.

Bilbo could not climb well. Dori helped him up into a tree. The wolves sat under the trees and howled. They waited for the goblins to come.

Night fell. The goblins arrived with torches. They laughed when they saw the dwarves and the hobbit in the trees. They began to set fire to the trees one by one. The flames grew higher. The heat and smoke became terrible. It seemed that the company would be burned or forced to jump down among the wolves.

Just when everything looked hopeless, great shapes appeared in the sky. They were eagles, the great eagles of the mountains. The Lord of the Eagles had seen the fire from far away. He hated the goblins and the wargs. He and his eagles swooped down.

The eagles picked up the dwarves and Bilbo and Gandalf one by one. They carried them high into the air, away from the fire and the enemies. The goblins and wargs howled with rage below.

The eagles flew a long way. At last they set the company down on a wide, rocky shelf high on the mountains, far from the danger. The Lord of the Eagles spoke with Gandalf. Then the eagles brought food — sheep and other meat — and left them for the night.

Bilbo was exhausted. He slept curled up on the hard rock more soundly than he had ever slept in his soft bed at home. All night he dreamed of his own house. In his dream he walked through all his different rooms, looking for something he could not find and could not remember.
